## What this trailer actually shows
This Cinema Guild trailer for *Umberto Eco: A Library of the World* (dir. Davide Ferrario, 2022) is built almost entirely from **testimonial/interview segments filmed in other libraries and archives**, plus stock engravings, a miniature diorama, the Cenacolo Vinciano, and several iconic "grand library" establishing shots (a Wiblingen-style Rococo hall, Stuttgart City Library, Tianjin Binhai Library, Biblioteca Vasconcelos, an unidentified vaulted archive, and a card-catalog reading room). Only a handful of shots are genuinely inside Eco's Milan flat:

- **Corridor / study (B&W archival footage, `t_000003`–`t_000015`)**: Eco walking away from camera between floor-to-ceiling white shelves into a study with a rolling ladder and a table piled with books. Heavy motion blur and small scale make every spine illegible even after 2x/sharpened tiling — recorded as `unlabelled` counts.
- **Study philosophy shelf (`t_000039` / `s_000039_12`)** — the one genuinely rich reading in this video: a personal shelf of Bompiani's "Il Pensiero Occidentale" series (Locke, Hume, Berkeley, Montesquieu, Voltaire, Vico, Kant x2, Santayana) mixed with Peanuts figurines (Charlie Brown, Snoopy, Schroeder, Linus) and Eco's own paper shelf-tags (L11.5, L11.6, L12.5, L12.6).
- **Eco browsing/reading his own shelf (`t_000107`–`t_000111`)**: shallow depth of field keeps spines out of focus; unlabelled.
- **Convex-mirror reflection (`s_000111_46`)**: a distorted glimpse of a different, wood-furnished room with a glass-fronted cabinet — tentatively the living room, flagged rather than asserted.

A close shelf of six 17th-century **Kircher** first editions (*Turris Babel*, *Ars Magna Lucis et Umbrae*, *Ars Magna Sciendi*, *China Illustrata*, *Obeliscus Pamphilius*, *Mundus Subterraneus* — `t_000051`/`t_000053`) is clearly legible and plausibly Eco's own copies, but the plain dark shelving shown (no glass-fronted cherry cabinets) and its place in an archive-heavy sequence kept it flagged `room_id: other` rather than the flat's rare-book room.
